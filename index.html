How a change goes

Trackload never talks to an LLM itself. It writes the brief, reads the reply, and leaves the asking to you, in whichever chat app you like.

  1. Say what should change

    Type it the way you would tell a coach. Copy for LLM puts your request on the clipboard with everything an assistant needs to answer it.

  2. Barbell rows keep missing and my lower back is sore. Swap them for T-bar rows, and take bench down 10% while my shoulder settles.+ plan, weights and training
    Here is the plan with T-bar rows in place of barbell rows on Upper Power, starting at 70 kg, and bench taken down 10%.
    ```trackload
trackload plan 1
program "Power & Hypertrophy"
…

    You copy the whole reply

    Ask in your chat app

    Paste it into the assistant you already use. It replies with the whole plan, written in Trackload's plan language.

  3. Paste the reply, then decide

    Trackload works out what changed and lists it in plain words, as in the review at the top of this page. Apply it, or discard it and nothing happens. A reply with a mistake in it shows the line and what is wrong, ready to copy back to the assistant.

The next session is already right

Bench was 92.5 kg. After the change, Today has it at 82.5, rounded to plates, and the T-bar row at the 70 kg the reply started it on.

Your logged sessions are never touched. A change only reaches the plan and the weights you are about to lift.

A plan language both of you can read

The plan is plain text: days, exercises, sets, reps and how the weight moves. The brief Trackload copies explains every rule of it, so the assistant writes valid plans without being taught.

  • I can only train three days a week now.schedule mon wed fri
  • Reset deadlift to 140 and build back up.deadlift: working weight 140
  • Add a fourth set of overhead press.overhead press 4x5-8
  • Build me a four-day upper/lower for strength.program "Upper / Lower"

New programs, or the one you run

Ask for a program from scratch, or for changes to the one you are on. A change to one of the twelve built-in programs lands on your own copy; the library's version stays as it was.

Overrides when you need them

One session lighter, a weight held until a date, a training max reset, a deload now. Each is a line in the plan's adjust block, and the review says what it does to the number before it applies.

Nothing guessed

An exercise the library does not know becomes a custom one, and if the reply leaves out its muscles, equipment or movement, Trackload asks you before applying. A number out of range, or a line it cannot read, is reported by line instead of half applied.

By hand, too

A plan can also be edited in the app, free and without limit: days, exercises, sets, reps, weight jumps, the progression rule, starting weights and the schedule.

On a Mac, the assistant can edit the file

With iCloud backup on, Trackload keeps a Trackload folder in your iCloud Drive: the program you run as a text file, every program you have made, your current weights and every set you have logged, as CSV.

An assistant on your Mac that can open files reads your history and rewrites program.txt itself. Open Trackload on your phone and the edit is waiting as a review, the same one a pasted reply gets.

Between changes, the program runs itself

The LLM changes the plan when you ask. The rest of the time, Trackload's progression engine decides every next weight: the jump after a good session, the hold after a miss, the deload after repeated ones.

Twelve programs, carried whole
Linear progressions, upper/lower and push/pull/legs splits, and percentage waves, each with its own percentage table.
Linear
A fixed jump every session, until a miss.
Linear with AMRAP
The last set goes as far as it will, and a big one earns a double jump.
Double progression
Reps climb inside a range first; the weight moves once every set reaches the top.
Waves
Percentages of a training max across the weeks of a cycle, then the max moves. A higher-volume variant for programs built on accumulated work.
Tiered
A heavy tier, a volume tier and an accessory tier, each progressing on its own terms.
